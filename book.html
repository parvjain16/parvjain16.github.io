My Reading List

Books that have shaped my thinking on technology, business, and life

Books I've Read

Hover, focus, or tap a book to discover its story.

Peter Thiel explores how startups can create something genuinely new instead of competing to copy what already exists. It is a guide to original thinking, innovation, and building a lasting business.

Zero to One: Notes on Startups, Or How to Build the Future

Peter Thiel with Blake Masters

Ben Horowitz shares the difficult decisions behind building and running a company, from hiring and layoffs to surviving a crisis. The book focuses on leadership when there are no easy answers.

The Hard Thing About Hard Things

Ben Horowitz

James Clear explains how small, consistent changes can grow into lasting results. It offers practical systems for building good habits and breaking unwanted ones.

Atomic Habits: An Easy & Proven Way to Build Good Habits & Break Bad Ones

James Clear

Jack Welch shares practical lessons on leadership, teamwork, competition, and career decisions. It is a hands-on guide to running stronger organizations and doing better work.

Winning

Jack Welch with Suzy Welch

The leaders behind Google share lessons from Bill Campbell, the coach who helped shape Silicon Valley. It shows how trust, honest feedback, and putting people first can build stronger teams.

Trillion Dollar Coach: The Leadership Handbook of Silicon Valley's Bill Campbell

Eric Schmidt, Jonathan Rosenberg & Alan Eagle

William Poundstone traces the history of game theory through John von Neumann and the Cold War. It explores why cooperation and self-interest can lead people into difficult strategic choices.

Prisoner's Dilemma

William Poundstone

Books on My List

The next ideas I'm looking forward to exploring.

Robert Greene uses historical stories to examine how power is gained, maintained, and lost. It explores influence, strategy, and the tactics people use to navigate competitive situations.

The 48 Laws of Power

Robert Greene

Reed Hastings and Erin Meyer explain the culture of freedom and responsibility behind Netflix. It explores how candid feedback, talented teams, and fewer controls can support innovation.

No Rules Rules: Netflix and the Culture of Reinvention

Reed Hastings & Erin Meyer

Hamilton Helmer lays out seven sources of enduring competitive advantage. It explains how companies turn inventions into businesses that stay valuable over time.

7 Powers: The Foundations of Business Strategy

Hamilton Helmer

David Deutsch explores how better explanations create knowledge and drive progress. It connects science, creativity, and optimism to the possibility of solving ever more difficult problems.

The Beginning of Infinity

David Deutsch

Tony Fadell draws on his experience with the iPod, iPhone, and Nest to share advice on making meaningful products. It covers design, leadership, startups, and the hard decisions behind building something new.

Build

Tony Fadell

Ben Horowitz argues that company culture comes from everyday decisions and behavior, not slogans. Drawing on history and modern businesses, he explains how leaders can shape culture deliberately.

What You Do Is Who You Are: How to Create Your Business Culture

Ben Horowitz
